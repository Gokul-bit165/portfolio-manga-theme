# Anime Background Pipeline

**Written for the owner.** How a frame is layered, and what exists today.

| Layer | Representation | State |
|---|---|---|
| Sky | A cloud card painted on a 2D canvas in a worker, on a gradient. Cirrus as tapered strokes, masses as overlapping dabs with a glazed underside | **Built** for the Muster Field. The masses are too airbrushed; they need firmer, more varied edges |
| Far distance | Rings of ridgeline at four depths, each with its own parallax | **Built.** Painted light and shadow planes, snow, haze at the foot |
| Middle ground | Selective 3D: tents, palisade, trees, standards | **Built,** simple shapes; the weakest layer |
| Foreground | 3D: road, grass, racks, carts, tufts | **Built,** sparse |
| Near framing (branches, a ruined wall across the frame) | Foreground silhouettes | **Not built.** Likely the cheapest large gain in depth |

## Why the sky is not a flat backdrop

The far ranges are real rings at several distances, so they slide against each other as the camera moves. The cloud card itself sits at infinity and only drifts very slowly.

## No image from your references enters the pipeline

Every cloud, ridge and stroke is generated by script. Nothing is traced or sampled from a reference.
