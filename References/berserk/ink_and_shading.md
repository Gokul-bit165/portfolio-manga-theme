# Ink and Shading

Tags: **[O]** observed, **[M]** measured, **[I]** interpretation. Citation format is in the [README](README.md#citation-format).

## Summary

1. Black and white are both used at full strength, and darkness depends on their contrast.
2. Detail is unevenly placed: around edges, in shadow, and at focal points. Lit surfaces stay almost empty.
3. Materials are separated by the *direction and type* of mark.
4. Shape is decided first. Hatching models forms that already read.

Definitions, on a 0 to 255 scale at 480 px page width:

- **Near-black** is below 50, and **near-white** is above 205.

### How V3 compares

- **[M]** V3 is darker on average than every panel measured. Its mean is 36, against 43 for the star field and 77 or more for the rest.
- **[M]** No V3 pixel exceeds 205, and 99.9% are at or below 141. Its 95th percentile is 86.
- **[M]** The only panel with no white (v34 p6) is a knocked-back recap background, with black plates of text over it.
- **[O]** The panels that feel darkest get that feeling from solid black next to bright shapes: stone against black sky on v33 p141 (35% black, 31% white), and a nave with pale openings on v33 p96.
- **[I]** V3 reads as underexposed where the references read as high contrast. A film image is not ink on paper, and need not match these numbers, but it should have a real highlight structure. The ink pass should aim at the panels' range.

**Caution:** comparing a continuous-tone render with bi-level ink is imperfect. The value bands are a tool for discussion, not a scorecard.

## Black distribution

- **[O]** Blacks gather into a few big connected shapes: cape (v01 p91), hill (v20 p166), hair (v22 p86), or sky (v33 p141).
- **[O]** Black sky reverses the usual relationship, leaving stone almost white (v33 p141).
- **[O]** The same subject can be drawn at either extreme. v34 p6 shows a tower in mid-tone, and v34 p36 shows it in black above a pale fog band.
- **[O]** Thin white lines separate overlapping blacks (v01 p91 cape folds, v31 p66 armour).
- **[I]** Solid black is treated as a compositional shape rather than a shadow calculation. Shadows are placed where they help the picture.

- **[O]** Not everything is cross-hatched. In the crops I studied, parallel hatching and tone are more common, and true multi-directional hatching is kept for the deepest shadows.
- **[O]** Line weight varies between thick outer contour and fine interior detail (v07 p157, v33 p141).

## Fine line and texture variation

- **[O]** Material contrast within a single panel: v33 p156 has curved brick ticks behind straight timber scaffolding. v29 p168 has a plain wall, clumped ivy and fine grass.
- **[O]** Smooth against dense: the black figure on v34 p171 has almost no surface marks and stands in front of very busy root forms.
- **[O]** A single unusually smooth surface stands out: the toned, soft-looking face at v01 p216 (from the survey sheet).
- **[I]** Readers read texture contrast as material contrast. Busy next to plain is more convincing than busy everywhere.

## How detailed environments stay readable

This is the key lesson for V3.

- **[O]** Stone surfaces are largely left empty. On v33 p141 walls are suggested by scattered short ticks, more of them toward shadow and corners.
- **[O]** v29 p168 is nearly blank wall with only vertical cracks and ivy clumps.
- **[O]** Early work outlines more stones. v01 p11 draws rounded blocks fully, with hatch on their edges. Later pages suggest more and draw less.
- **[O]** Big shapes are clear before detail begins. Even dense pages such as v21 p112 keep pale figures against darker hatching.
- **[O]** Value bands stay separate: v01 p151 keeps fog, tree line and foreground distinct.
- **[I]** Rules for readable density:
  1. One value per large shape.
  2. Detail only at edges, shadow transitions, and focal points.
  3. Different mark direction for different materials.
  4. Keep at least one large quiet area per image.

## Ink test for any future image

**[I]** Apply a hard threshold at three levels (dark, mid, light), and ask:

1. Is the main subject still identifiable at every level?
2. Are the shapes large and few, or broken into specks?
3. Is there a clear brightest shape, and is it where the eye should go?
4. Is there a calm area?

V3 already fails question 2 at the light threshold, where the wall breaks into specks. See [blender_translation.md](blender_translation.md).
