# Architecture and Environment

Tags: **[O]** observed, **[M]** measured, **[I]** interpretation. Citation format is in the [README](README.md#citation-format).

## Overview

Buildings here are not background decoration. They tell the reader who has power and how small a person is. They are mostly drawn from real architectural types, but this is my visual reading, since the pages do not name any sources.

**Limit of this sample:** I saw many intact buildings, and few true masonry ruins. Ruin advice below is partly extrapolated and marked.

## How stone is drawn

The approach changes noticeably over the volumes.

| Period | Stone treatment | Pages |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Rounded blocks fully outlined, hatched at their edges | [O] v01 p11 |
| Early, in shade | Ruled vertical hatching over the surface, plus tone | [O] v02 p199 |
| Golden Age | Fine ruled line, regular courses, thin marks | [O] v06 p148, v07 p139 |
| Late | Short scattered ticks that imply blocks, thicker near corners and shadow | [O] v33 p141 |
| Late, massive | Directional coursing that describes the curve of a form | [O] v20 p22, v33 p156 |
| Late, weathered | Mostly blank surface with vertical cracks and ivy clumps | [O] v29 p168 |

**[I]** Later stone has less drawing per block, and more information overall. The eye fills in the coursing from a few marks.

### What this means for V3

**[I]** V3 applies mottling and joints evenly to all faces. The references leave lit faces nearly empty and concentrate marks at edges and shadow. So the fix for V3's "stone looks too even" is likely *redistribution*, not more surface noise.

## Stone arches

- **[O]** Arches often appear as openings framed by dark, and with something at the end. On v06 p148 a dark arch and small figure close a deep hall.
- **[O]** On v14 p211 tall arched windows are bright behind dark piers, and the arch shape is defined by light.
- **[O]** On v33 p96 pale openings run up a black wall, with a small dark figure in a pale arch at the base.
- **[O]** On v02 p190 an arched window is shown strictly frontally, framed by black, with a figure in it.
- **[O]** On v24 p134 a soft arch-like pale area sits behind the dark back view of a figure.
- **[O]** Arches repeat to build rhythm (v06 p148, v33 p141 lower panels).
- **[I]** The arch works best as a **value shape**, either bright in dark or dark in bright. Stone detail on the arch itself is secondary. In V3 the opening is dark and surrounded by a mid-value wall, a weaker form of this idea.

## Scale

- **[O]** Small figures at the base of very tall spaces: v14 p211, v33 p96, v33 p156.
- **[O]** A walking group along a long wall: v29 p168.
- **[O]** Rows of tiny soldiers below a sky-high tower: v34 p36.
- **[O]** Looking sharply upward with strong convergence: v02 p199, v33 p141.
- **[O]** Repeating units make size legible, since viewers can count courses, arches, or windows: v06 p148, v33 p141, v21 p118.
- **[I]** V3 has no figure and no repeating unit large enough to read. Its arch could be 3 m or 30 m. A scale reference is the cheapest possible upgrade.

## Atmospheric depth in environments

- **[O]** v01 p151 stacks bands: tone sky, bright cloud, pale fog, toned tree line, pale fog, dark tree line, and near-black conifers.
- **[O]** v22 p166 shows huge figures fading to paler grey the further they stand, above a white blaze.
- **[O]** v07 p139 dissolves a cliff behind a fortress using spatter.
- **[O]** v34 p36 uses a pale cloud band to separate a black tower from the plain.
- **[I]** Depth is built with **hard-edged bands at separated values**. Each layer has one value and a shaped top edge. This is different from a smooth, uniform fog.

## Weathering and growth

- **[O]** v29 p168: long stone wall, plain surface, ivy in clumps at top and along cracks, overgrown path and hedge beyond.
- **[O]** v14 p139: rough wall top, individually outlined stones, pale.
- **[I]** Growth is clustered, and it interrupts long plain surfaces. Spreading moss evenly would lose this.

## Lighting and shadow in architecture

- **[O]** Black sky, near-white stone (v33 p141).
- **[O]** Black interior, bright openings (v33 p96, v24 p150).
- **[O]** Day scenes rely on deep shade under eaves and arches rather than dark surfaces (v01 p11).
- **[O]** Fire as near-white with the building drawn only by dark details inside it (v26 p132).

## Ruins (partly extrapolated)

**[I]** Applying the principles above to a ruin:

1. Break the skyline with a few large clear gaps, not an even staircase of small steps.
2. Keep large wall areas plain, and put damage at corners and edges.
3. Use clumped growth at the top and in breaks.
4. Let collapse leave piles with varied sizes and strong shadow, kept to a few places.
5. Separate far ruins with pale bands.

V3's stepped wall tops and pixel-like distant silhouettes show what to avoid.

## Originality rules for the portfolio world

- Use real historic building types directly as reference, such as photographs of actual medieval architecture, rather than deriving from manga panels.
- Do not reuse these specific ideas: a tower with branching limbs in the sky (v34 p6), mass-of-faces structures (v20 p166), or the rows of hanging forms (v33 p96).
- Each location should come from the portfolio's own story. See [portfolio_art_direction.md](portfolio_art_direction.md#chapter-by-chapter-direction).
