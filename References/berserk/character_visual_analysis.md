# Character Visual Analysis

Tags: **[O]** observed, **[M]** measured, **[I]** interpretation. Citation format is in the [README](README.md#citation-format).

This records principles for an original character. It is not a design guide for reproducing anyone. The closing lists say what must not be borrowed.

## Part 1: The swordsman

### Silhouette and physical presence

- **[O]** On v01 p91 he is shown from behind, full body, filling the page. The cape is almost solid black, and the sword is a long plank running behind his leg. Without the interior lines, the outline would still identify him.
- **[O]** On v14 p166 the camera is low and he towers over a small child in the foreground. The sword rests on his shoulder and runs off the page, and the cape sweeps around him as one curved black mass.
- **[O]** The back view recurs: v01 p91, v15 p46, v24 p134. In v24 p134 he stands dark against a pale arch-shaped area.
- **[I]** The silhouette is built from three contrasting things: a soft, heavy mass (cape), one rigid straight bar (sword), and a small spiky top (hair). Soft against rigid is what makes it memorable, not the amount of detail.
- **[I]** Repeated back views mean the reader often follows him rather than meets him. This creates distance and suits a wanderer.

### Armour and clothing

- **[O]** Early plate and belt detail (v01 p91) is fairly simple: straps, pouches, tall boots with fur tops, and a tattered cape hem.
- **[O]** The later armour (v27 p60, v31 p66) is nearly solid black, and is read almost entirely through thin white highlights that follow its facets. The helm crop on v31 p66 is mostly black with a few bright slashes.
- **[O]** On v07 p157, a different character's polished plate shows the opposite approach: each plate has a pale centre, hatch fans at both ends, and solid black where plates overlap.
- **[I]** Two armour languages exist: *lit plate* (mid-values, shows construction) and *black armour* (a shape with scratched-in light). The second reads as threat because the viewer cannot see how it works.

### Sword proportions and visual weight

- **[O]** On v01 p91 the sword spans roughly head-height hand to ankle. It is a flat bar with a pale inner line.
- **[O]** On v06 p121 the earlier sword is long but narrow, with a crossguard, and it crosses the whole page as a clean diagonal.
- **[O]** On v24 p134 it is strapped diagonally across the back, and on v14 p166 it leaves the frame.
- **[O]** Against the black cape, the blade often reads as the palest large shape in that area (v01 p91).
- **[I]** Weight is communicated by what the object does to the body and the frame: it is carried across the shoulder, it rests against the ground, it breaks the panel. Scale alone is not the point.

### Face and expression

- **[O]** v17 p60: a frontal face floats in a completely black page, with one white speech balloon above. Hatching appears only at the edges of the face and under the cheekbones. One eye is reduced to a narrow slit.
- **[O]** v22 p86: an extreme close-up, half a page, of one eye, with black bars of hair crossing it. The white of the eye is the brightest shape.
- **[O]** v01 p91 top panel: the same device early on, with the face covered in dense hatching and one clean eye.
- **[O]** v28 p24: a large three-quarter face with a fierce grin, hatching following the planes of the face, and a pale streak in the hair. The streak is also visible on v33 p51.
- **[O]** v28 p78: strain is shown by a sweating face at a tilted angle, teeth bared, close to the frame edge.
- **[O]** v29 p132: at rest, without armour, eyes closed, shoulders and scars lightly drawn. The tone is calm.
- **[I]** The eye is the character's signature device. When a page shows only one bright eye within darkness, it reads as intent with no need for an expression.

### How exhaustion, determination and isolation are communicated

| Quality | How it appears | Pages |
|---|---|---|
| Exhaustion | Sweat, head dropping low in the frame, eyes closed, armour removed | v28 p78, v29 p132, v33 p216 |
| Determination | Single eye, frontal stare, jaw set, back turned to the viewer | v17 p60, v22 p86, v24 p134 |
| Isolation | Background removed entirely, or replaced by a flat tone | v17 p60, v01 p91 |
| Isolation by scale | Tiny figure with the whole page between him and someone else | v22 p38, v15 p46 |
| Damage over time | Scars, bandaging, iron hardware, pale hair streak | v28 p24, v29 p132, v33 p51 |

- **[I]** Isolation is often made by *removing* drawing. The most alone he looks is on v17 p60, which has the least environment in the sample.

### Before and after the Eclipse

Page order matters: the reader meets the later version first (v01), then sees the younger one (late v03 onward).

| Aspect | Before (v06, v07, v13 p175) | After (v01 to v03, v14 onward) |
|---|---|---|
| Line | Clean outline, little hatching on the figure | Dense hatching, solid blacks |
| Figure value | Pale, near the page's value | Dark mass, often the darkest shape |
| Clothing | Light tunic, partial plate, short cape | Heavy black cape, later black armour |
| Sword | Long and narrow (v06 p121) | Broad slab (v01 p91, v28 p78) |
| Posture | Sitting at ease, open, looking away (v06 p121) | Set, advancing or braced (v14 p166) |
| Eyes | Wide and readable (v06 p31, v13 p175) | Shadowed, narrowed, or one bright eye |
| Body marks | Few visible | Scars, hardware, bandages (v33 p51) |

- **[M]** The figure's change matches the pages around it. Near-black coverage is 8.5% in held Golden Age pages and 15.1% in Black Swordsman pages. See [ink_and_shading.md](ink_and_shading.md#measured-distribution-by-arc).
- **[O]** v13 p175 shows him running in light armour in a pale column of the page, while the rest of the page is black.
- **[I]** The change is a change in *value*, as much as in costume. A character can be made to feel transformed by moving them from pale to dark in the page's value structure.
- **Limitation:** volumes 8 to 12 are missing, so the steps in between were not seen.

## Part 2: The companion and emotional contrast

### Observations

- **[O]** Almost no hatching on the body. On v01 p151 the small fairy is drawn in clean outline with large wet eyes, on a page whose landscape is dense tone and hatching.
- **[O]** Separation by halo. On v22 p102 the pale head sits against dense dark demon detail, kept readable by a pale edge. v02 p199 shows a similar pale rim against a toned tower.
- **[O]** Tiny scale. The figure usually occupies a small part of the panel, sits on someone's shoulder, or flies at the edge (v25 p96, v15 p172).
- **[O]** Two drawing systems. A fuller form (v16 p158 top) and a very simple rounded form with minimal features and an open-mouth shout (v16 p158 bottom, v26 p96, v15 p172, v24 p134). Both can occur on the same page. The earliest simple form I saw is v15 p172, but I did not search for the first occurrence.
- **[O]** Interruption of tension. On v26 p96 the simple round form appears next to an intense fight panel. v24 p134 places a soft comic panel above a heavy back view of the swordsman, on one page.
- **[O]** Real feeling too. On v01 p151 the crying is played straight, not as a joke, and v01 p161 ends a chapter on the fairy alone.
- **[O]** Humour is not limited to the companion. v24 p64 shows gentle, round, furry creatures with spiral faces in a fully textured landscape.
- **[O]** Other soft-faced characters do similar work. Round faces drawn with few lines appear next to dark forms on v27 p24, v31 p66 and v33 p216.

### Principles for an original companion

| Principle | Reason | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Lightest drawing on the page | It reads instantly against dense art | [O] v01 p151 |
| Silhouette opposite to the lead | Round and small against tall and angular | [I] from v24 p134 |
| Keep a pale edge around it | Stays legible on any background | [O] v22 p102 |
| Two scales of expression | One detailed form, one extremely simple form for reactions | [O] v16 p158 |
| Use it to release tension | Relief makes the dark moments stronger | [I] from v26 p96 |
| Let it be sincere sometimes | Otherwise it is only a joke device | [O] v01 p151 |
| Place it at the edge | It comments on the scene instead of competing with it | [O] v25 p96 |

## Part 3: Applying this without copying

### Transferable principles

1. Soft mass against one rigid object.
2. Value change as the sign of inner change.
3. Reserve the close-up. Show the eye or face rarely.
4. Back views for travelling, frontal views for confrontation.
5. Show accumulated wear on body and gear.
6. Lightest possible companion, with a two-tier drawing system.

### Must not be borrowed

**The lead:** oversized slab sword, black tattered cape as the main shape, short spiky hair, one damaged eye, prosthetic iron hand or arm with hardware, repeater crossbow, wolf-like spiked black armour, pale hair streak, bandaged-fist imagery.

**The companion:** small human-like fairy, leaf or insect wings, pointed elf ears, hair style, unclothed body, round-blob face with triangular ears.

**The poses:** the back view with raised weapon (v01 p91), floating face on black with a balloon above (v17 p60), low-angle hero over a child (v14 p166).

## Proposed direction for the originals

**[I]** All of this is a proposal for approval (roadmap decision D3).

### The Wanderer

The existing brief already describes a hooded traveller with a satchel, a heavy tool-staff and lantern, a patched cloak, and a face often in shadow. The research supports that, with these refinements:

- **Silhouette:** top-heavy and asymmetric. A tall frame pack with scroll tubes and tools rises above one shoulder. This reads differently from a cape and a sword at any size.
- **Rigid element:** a long iron surveyor's staff, the one straight bar in the shape. It works as a walking staff, a measuring tool and a weapon if needed.
- **Soft element:** a patched, hooded travelling coat in *mid-value*, with visible seams and repairs. Not a black cape.
- **Posture:** leaning forward slightly under the load, in contrast with the upright, braced source figure.
- **Face:** hood shadow until the final scene, as `planning/STORYBOARD.md` already proposes. Eyes visible only at key moments.
- **Change over the story:** the reverse of the source. Gear is *repaired and added to*, not destroyed. Value moves from dark to lighter as understanding grows, in step with the monochrome to colour arc.

### The Spark

**Recommended change:** drop the moth idea from the current brief. Moth and butterfly-winged small beings appear in the source (v14 p184 from the survey sheet, v16 p116 from the survey sheet), so that design sits too close.

**Proposal:** a small round glass-bodied lamp creature, somewhat like a beetle.

- **Shape:** a round body on stub legs, no wings, no human anatomy. It is the visual opposite of the Wanderer.
- **Expression:** two dot eyes and a shutter-like lid over the light. Detailed form for hero moments, a simple two-stroke version for reactions.
- **Drawing:** clean line, no hatching, keeps a pale edge.
- **Colour role:** it is the first and only gold object in the monochrome chapters, so the colour arc starts with it. This idea is not from the source.
- **Role:** short mono captions, as already specified. Humour and small sincere moments.
- **Check needed:** avoid resemblance to well-known living-flame or soot-creature characters from other works, and to any known lamp mascot.
