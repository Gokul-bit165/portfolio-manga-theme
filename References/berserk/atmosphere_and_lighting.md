### Black Swordsman

- **[O]** Dense surface hatching across figures, and tone shapes standing in for background (v01 p91).
- **[O]** Bright day can be just as hostile. v01 p11 is a pale, bright town, yet it opens a violent story.
- **[O]** Monsters fill the frame as textured masses (v03 p41, from the survey sheet).
- **[I]** Grit here comes from line, not black area. Near-black is high, but solid ink share is low (3.6%).

### Golden Age

- **[O]** Clean figures, more white, plain line backgrounds: v06 p121, v06 p139.
- **[O]** Big organised forces and ceremony: v07 p31, v07 p184, v07 p157.
- **[O]** Warm scenes in ink terms: v07 p121 has small figures in a pale clearing ringed by black foliage.
- **[O]** Fortress as a pale drawing with little shadow: v07 p139.
- **[I]** This is the source's "hope" look. Pale values and clear lines signal a future that is still open. It does more emotional work because the reader has already seen v01.

### Eclipse

- **[O]** Pure black used as cosmic space, with spiral marks in white (v13 p67).
- **[O]** Near-empty pages used for shock (v13 p49 from the survey sheet).
- **[O]** Page structure itself breaks down: v13 p175.
- **[O]** Horizons vanish on the survey sheets I saw (v13 p148, v13 p157), and depth is replaced by pattern.
- **[O]** The aftermath returns to ordinary daylight, cumulus sky and rural scenes on v14 p31.
- **[I]** The horror is partly a loss of space. Normal ground, horizon and panel order are removed, and the return of clouds becomes emotional relief.

### Conviction

- **[O]** Black as a stage for pale forms on v15 p46.
- **[O]** Huge dark form above a mass of faces on v20 p166.
- **[O]** Masses of people as texture on v18 p46 and v17 p102.
- **[O]** Soft hope is kept in reserve: v16 p102 has two small figures in flowers under a big sky.
- **[O]** A stormy, oppressive sky on v20 p22.
- **[M]** v20 is the darkest volume in the whole set.
- **[I]** Darkness here is social and physical at once, since crowds and institutions press in. It feels heavy without any cosmic imagery.

### Falcon of the Millennium Empire

- **[O]** Enormous scale in sky and distance: v34 p6, v34 p36.
- **[O]** Wonder and calm landscapes alongside danger: v23 p102, v23 p166, v23 p182.
- **[O]** Black armour as a pure graphic form: v31 p66, v27 p60.
- **[O]** Highly detailed cities: v33 p141, v33 p156, v29 p96.
- **[O]** Magical light is drawn with pale marks on pale ground: v25 p78, v31 p186.
- **[O]** Comic and gentle characters are more common: v24 p64, v24 p134.
- **[M]** Near-black coverage (10.5%) is lower than in the Black Swordsman, Eclipse and Conviction segments, and spreads are more common (9.1% of pages).
- **[I]** Atmosphere shifts toward awe. The threat becomes geographical and mythic, and pale pages are often the most unsettling ones.

## Darkness

Types of darkness I could distinguish:

| Type | How it is made | Feeling | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid black stage | Flat black with pale forms | Isolation, ritual | [O] v15 p46, v17 p60 |
| Black with specks | White dots or marks scattered on black | Vastness | [O] v13 p67, v23 p182 |
| Black architecture | Walls gone solid, openings bright | Oppression | [O] v33 p96 |
| Black sky | Bright buildings against it | Night clarity, menace | [O] v33 p141 |
| Hatched darkness | Dense lines with some visible paper | Grit, closeness | [O] v01 p91, v21 p112 |
| Silhouette against light | Dark figures at a bright opening | Threat, mystery | [O] v24 p150 |
| Tone darkness | Grey plate, low contrast | Uneasy quiet | [O] v34 p6, v01 p161 |

- **[I]** Pure black gets used in a few recognisable ways, each with its own feeling. "Make it darker" is too vague a direction. The useful question is which of these darknesses a scene needs.

## Fog and haze

- **[O]** Fog bands, pale and with shaped edges, between tree lines (v01 p151).
- **[O]** A bright band separating a black tower from a plain (v34 p36).
- **[O]** Cauliflower smoke, drawn by outlines and hatch at the edges, with small dark specks for scale (v21 p166, v20 p22).
- **[O]** Spatter that dissolves a far cliff (v07 p139).
- **[O]** Heat and fire haze: figures fade to grey by distance (v22 p166).
- **[I]** Fog in this work is a shape with an edge, and it has an important job: separating values. V3's fog is correct but quiet, and it mostly lowers contrast.

## Light

- **[O]** Rays cut out of tone by removing it, leaving white shafts below a cloud (v01 p151). The sky has to be a mid-tone first.
- **[O]** Fan-shaped shafts over a dark form (v20 p166).
- **[O]** A bright crackling light on rock with a figure at low angle (v17 p186).
- **[O]** Candle clusters as many small light sources (v20 p86).
- **[O]** Fire as near-pure white (v26 p132).
- **[O]** Backlit window arches on stone floor gradient (v14 p211).
- **[O]** A moon disc behind a small silhouette (v20 p22).
- **[O]** Rim light as a thin white contour around a dark form (v01 p91, v15 p46).
- **[I]** Light is used as shape. It is usually the white of the page, and it needs something dark or mid-value around it to exist.

## Quiet and relief

- **[O]** Clouds after horror: v14 p31.
- **[O]** Field and flowers: v16 p102.
- **[O]** Marsh with a comic moment: v15 p172.
- **[O]** Stars: v23 p182.
- **[I]** Relief scenes are drawn with as much care as violent ones. They make the world worth caring about, and they are a natural model for your later chapters.

## Editorial use of darkness

Possibly publisher additions rather than the artist's. Still relevant for the site.

- **[O]** Black chapter opener with a small title: v24 p102, v26 p24, v32 p96, v34 p96.
- **[O]** A knocked-back grey image with wide-spaced title letters: v32 p51, v33 p51.
- **[O]** Knocked-back art with a solid black text plate: v32 p6, v33 p6, v34 p6.
- **[I]** The recap plates solve the exact problem in your design rules, "text always on a plate." They show that this looks right in this world, and it doesn't feel like a web convention.

## Principles to take forward

**[I]**

1. Name the type of darkness per chapter.
2. Keep at least one bright shape in every dark image.
3. Build atmosphere from bands of separate value.
4. Use light where it has mid-tone or dark to cut against.
5. Plan relief scenes with equal effort.
6. Use loss of ground and order only as a rare event.
7. Let the arc from dense to pale carry meaning.
